# Romy and the Banana

Romy found a banana on the kitchen table. It was bright as a little sun, and it had a tiny smile where the peel folded.

“Hello,” said the banana. “I am Ban. I have never seen the garden.”

Romy tucked Ban into her pocket, careful not to squash him. They went out the back door. The grass was cool. A bee hummed past like a tiny engine.

“The world is very loud,” whispered Ban.

“It is only being friendly,” said Romy.

They walked to the hedge. A snail was crossing the path, slow as a secret. Romy waited. Ban waited. The snail reached the other side and lifted its feelers, as if to say thank you.

Beyond the hedge was the lane. A puddle held a scrap of sky. Romy crouched so Ban could look.

“That is the whole blue,” said Ban, very proud.

A gust of wind tried to steal Ban from the pocket. Romy held him tight. “We go home the same way we came,” she said. “That is how adventures stay safe.”

In the kitchen she set Ban back on the table, next to the fruit bowl. He was a little dusty from the lane. Romy wiped him with a tea towel until he shone.

“I saw grass,” said Ban. “I saw a snail. I saw the sky in a puddle.”

“Tomorrow,” said Romy, “we can see the park.”

Ban’s little smile grew. Outside, the bee went on humming, and the garden kept the secret of their walk, as gardens do.
